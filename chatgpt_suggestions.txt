novel or a copy 

I inspected the repository, its RELATED_WORK.md, the core TernaryLoRALinear implementation, and the closest prior papers/projects. My assessment is:

Verdict

It is not a copy. But it is also not novel in the broad sense of “a ternary VLM.”

The potentially publishable novelty is the particular PEFT/QAT formulation and its application to a natively ternary VLM, not the overall VLM architecture.

What is already known

Your repository is built from several established components:

BitNet b1.58 → ternary language-model weights.
SigLIP2 → vision encoder.
LLaVA-style projector + image-token insertion.
Pixel shuffle → reducing vision tokens.
LoRA → parameter-efficient adaptation.

Your README explicitly identifies these dependencies and prior techniques.

And ternary multimodal models already exist:

LLaVaOLMoBitnet1B was published in August 2024 and demonstrated a ternary LLM combined with a vision encoder and projector.

BitVLA was published in June 2025. Importantly, it uses BitNet b1.58 2B4T + SigLIP-L + MLP connector, which is extremely close to part of your architecture.

And by 2026, Ternary-Bonsai also provides ternary multimodal models, although its architecture and training regime are substantially different from yours.

So this claim would not be defensible:

“We introduce the first ternary VLM.”

That territory is already occupied.

Where your work actually becomes interesting

This part is much more specific.

Your core operation is effectively:

$$ W_{\mathrm{eff}}=W+sBA $$

followed by

$$ W_q=Q(W_{\mathrm{eff}}) $$

and the forward pass uses the quantized result itself, rather than doing ordinary LoRA on a quantized base:

$$ y=Q_a(x)Q_w(W+sBA)^T. $$

Your implementation explicitly does this and uses an STE for the quantizer.

That gives you the property you care about:

the model trained during QAT and the exported ternary model use the same quantized weights.

Your repository even tests that relationship bit-for-bit.

That is a real technical distinction from ordinary:

$$ Q(W)x + BAx $$

LoRA.

But here is the important problem

You should not claim that the mathematical idea of “put LoRA inside the quantizer” is completely new.

Two earlier methods are directly relevant:

L4Q — 2024

L4Q explicitly combines LoRA + quantization-aware training, with the low-rank update incorporated into the quantization process and producing a fully quantized model.

LR-QAT — 2024

LR-QAT also uses low-rank quantization-aware reparameterization, with the auxiliary low-rank parameters ultimately absorbed into the quantized weights so that inference has no additional adapter overhead.

Your own RELATED_WORK.md correctly recognizes this and says your TernaryLoRALinear is structurally related to these approaches.

So:

“We invented quantization-aware LoRA where \(BA\) is inside the quantizer.”

would be too strong.

Then what is potentially novel?

I would formulate the contribution much more narrowly:

Your research question

Can a pretrained natively ternary LLM be converted into a useful VLM using parameter-efficient adaptation while keeping the deployed language backbone strictly ternary and ensuring that the training-time and deployment-time quantized functions are identical?

That combination is substantially more interesting.

You are combining:

$$ \text{native ternary LM} + \text{vision adaptation} + \text{PEFT} + \text{QAT-aware low-rank adaptation} + \text{exact ternary export} $$

and, importantly, you are doing this without updating the full ternary backbone.

Your repository currently describes exactly that design: frozen SigLIP2, trained projector, ternary LoRA on BitNet 2B, and exact ternary export.

Your closest competitor is actually BitVLA

This is the comparison I would focus on in the paper.

	BitVLA	Your TernaVLM
Ternary LM	BitNet b1.58 2B4T	BitNet b1.58 2B4T
Vision encoder	SigLIP-L	SigLIP2
Projector	MLP	MLP
Multimodal	Yes	Yes
Main task	VLA / robotics	General VLM
LM adaptation	Training remaining model components	LoRA/QAT PEFT
Vision encoder	Eventually ternarized	Frozen FP vision encoder
Core contribution	1-bit VLA + ternary vision encoder	PEFT adaptation while retaining strict ternary LM
Exact ternary training/deployment match	Not the main contribution	Central contribution

BitVLA's paper says it starts with BitNet 2B4T and a full-precision vision encoder, then trains the connector and later performs further training; it subsequently introduces distillation-aware ternarization of the vision encoder.

So your paper shouldn't pretend BitVLA doesn't exist. Instead, it becomes the most important baseline against which you distinguish your approach.

Is your code a copy?

From what I inspected, I see no evidence that the repository is simply a copied implementation of LLaVaOLMoBitnet1B or BitVLA.

The repository has its own TernaryLoRALinear, ternary quantization/STE machinery, export/merge logic, tests, training pipeline, checkpoint handling, and Kaggle orchestration.

However, I would not claim “no copied code anywhere” merely from web inspection. That's a source-code provenance question requiring a line-by-line comparison against the relevant repositories.

What I can say is:

Research idea: derivative combination, but potentially novel contribution.
Architecture: largely assembled from existing components.
Core quantization mechanism: builds directly on prior QAT/LoRA literature.
Implementation: appears to contain substantial original engineering rather than being a straight copy.

The biggest issue right now

Your README currently says:

“all of them update the full set of latent weights with QAT.”

That sentence is risky. Your own related-work document is much more careful, but the README is making a broad claim.

I'd change the scientific positioning from:

“We created a new ternary VLM.”

to something closer to:

“We investigate parameter-efficient vision adaptation of a pretrained natively ternary language model, introducing a quantization-aware low-rank adaptation mechanism that preserves strict ternary deployment and eliminates train/deployment quantization mismatch.”

That is much more defensible.

And one thing will determine whether this is actually a paper

Right now your repo explicitly says there are no released weights and no benchmark results yet.

So at the moment, you have a research hypothesis and implementation, not yet a demonstrated scientific contribution.

The experiment that would make the argument compelling is roughly:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Plain LoRA} \quad vs\quad \text{LoRA + merge + requantization} \quad vs\quad \text{your QAT-LoRA} \quad vs\quad \text{full QAT} } $$

while measuring:

VLM accuracy + ternary mismatch + trainable parameters + GPU memory + CPU memory + CPU throughput.

That ablation would tell us whether your method is merely a recombination of known ideas or whether it produces a meaningful new result.

My current assessment:

Not a copy. Not a “new ternary VLM” either. Potentially a legitimate novel research contribution centered on parameter-efficient, quantization-aware adaptation of a native ternary LM for VLMs—but the novelty has to be demonstrated experimentally, especially against BitVLA, L4Q, and LR-QAT.

The reason is not the lack of a working implementation. It is novelty distance from existing work.

Your repo's central operation is:

$$ W_{\text{eff}} = W + sBA $$

followed by

$$ W_q = Q(W_{\text{eff}}) $$

with STE, and then exact export of the quantized merged tensor.

That is technically coherent, but the important problem is that QAT + low-rank adaptation is already established. L4Q introduced parameter-efficient QAT with LoRA in 2024, and LR-QAT independently developed low-rank QAT for LLMs.

More importantly, LoTA-QAF was accepted to the NeurIPS 2025 main conference and specifically addresses lossless ternary adaptation/merging of quantized LLMs.

So a reviewer asking:

"What is fundamentally new here compared with L4Q, LR-QAT and LoTA-QAF?"

is a very serious problem for a Tier-1 submission.

The VLM part does not solve that by itself

Ternary multimodal models already exist.

LLaVaOLMoBitnet1B introduced a ternary multimodal LLM in 2024.

BitVLA then used BitNet b1.58 2B4T + SigLIP and trained the model with QAT, producing a fully ternary VLA.

Your own related-work document correctly identifies this as the central competitive issue: TernaVLM uses the same BitNet 2B4T backbone, but changes the adaptation strategy to projector + LoRA and studies exact ternary export/CPU deployment.

That is a real difference, but at present it looks more like:

existing QAT/PEFT idea + existing ternary VLM architecture + a new combination/application

than:

a fundamentally new learning method.

For a Tier-1 main track, that distinction matters.

What would make it Tier-1 caliber?

The paper needs to answer a question that existing papers cannot already answer.

For example:

1. A genuinely new ternary adaptation algorithm

Not just:

$$ Q(W+BA) $$

but some mechanism specifically addressing a problem caused by ternary discretization.

For example, something theoretically or empirically justified around:

ternary transition dynamics,
zero-state instability,
scale collapse,
gradient starvation,
LoRA rank versus ternary state transitions,
how to efficiently move weights among \(-1,0,+1\),
preserving BitNet's activation/weight statistics during multimodal adaptation.

That would move the contribution from "application of QAT-PEFT" toward an actual new method.

2. Or a strong new empirical result

Suppose you demonstrate:

$$ \text{your method} \approx \text{full QAT accuracy} $$

while training, say, only 1–5% of parameters, using dramatically less GPU memory and compute, and then obtain a genuinely efficient CPU deployment.

That could itself be interesting—but the comparison must be extremely strong.

Your current planned ablations are going in the right direction:

projector only
ordinary FP LoRA
merge + requantization
your joint QAT-LoRA
full QAT
FP baseline

The repo already lists these experiments as future work.

But for Tier 1, I'd add L4Q / LR-QAT / LoTA-QAF-style baselines, not just your locally designed controls.

I would currently describe TernaVLM as:

A technically competent and potentially useful combination of existing ideas, with a specific unexplored application to parameter-efficient adaptation of a BitNet ternary VLM.

I would not yet call it a Tier-1-level research contribution.

The interesting part is that you are actually quite close to having a stronger paper. The gap isn't "build a much bigger model." The gap is:

$$ \boxed{\text{show something scientifically new about ternary adaptation}} $$

rather than merely showing that your implementation works.

And there is one especially important finding from the literature: LoTA-QAF already got a NeurIPS 2025 main-conference paper out of the ternary adaptation problem itself. That raises the novelty bar substantially for your work.

If I were evaluating the manuscript scientifically, the first thing I would demand before calling it Tier-1-ready is a direct technical comparison against L4Q, LR-QAT, LoTA-QAF, BitVLA, and full-QAT, with an explicit statement of exactly what mathematical property your method adds beyond them.
